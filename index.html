Universal Judges at ACM TRUST 2027

Reliable judging across languages and modalities

UniJudge is a full-day workshop at ACM TRUST 2027 asking whether AI judges stay reliable as the systems they evaluate move across languages, modalities and representations — spanning multilingual and low-resource judges, multimodal judges, their intersection, and cross-representation consistency.

Call for papers

AI judges increasingly shape model development, safety evaluations and deployment decisions. Their judgments need to remain reliable across languages, modalities and representations, yet changes in language, perceptual evidence or presentation can alter a verdict without changing the underlying quality. UniJudge brings together researchers in trustworthy AI, multilingual NLP, vision, speech and multimodal learning to audit these evaluators, compare failure modes and develop shared evaluation protocols.

We invite submissions on the reliability of AI judges in multilingual or multimodal settings, including methods, analyses, benchmarks and datasets. We also welcome work on reasoning validity, robustness, calibration, bias, causal faithfulness, judge disagreement and multi-judge systems with clear implications for these settings. Negative results, replications, position papers, preliminary findings and works in progress are explicitly welcome.

Long papers

Completed research: new methods, analyses, benchmarks, datasets or evaluation protocols for auditing and meta-evaluating judges.

Short papers

Position papers, negative results, replications, preliminary findings and works in progress.

Submission routes

We use a hybrid submission model, accepting both direct submissions via OpenReview and ACL Rolling Review (ARR) commitments. Previously published or accepted papers are eligible only for non-archival presentation.

Format

Direct submissions must be PDFs in the 2-column ACM sigconf template, following the TRUST 2027 author guidelines. References and the GenAI Usage Disclosure section do not count toward the page limit.

Review

Direct submissions are double-blind. Remove names, affiliations and identifying acknowledgments, and write self-references in the third person. Each direct submission receives at least two independent reviews from reviewers matched by expertise; organizers recuse themselves from conflicted submissions.

Presentation

At least one author of each accepted paper must register for ACM TRUST 2027 and present the work. The conference is hybrid, and remote presentation follows the conference’s virtual participation policies.

Archival publication

Details of archival publication options, including any fees or available waivers, will be announced once confirmed.

Student Spotlight

Accepted long and short papers whose first author is a student at the submission deadline may be considered for dedicated spotlight presentation slots. Indicate eligibility and interest when submitting.

Both archival and non-archival submissions are welcome. Previously published or accepted papers are eligible only for non-archival presentation.

Reciprocal reviewing

Each direct submission must nominate at least one qualified coauthor who agrees to review for the workshop if invited. A qualified reviewer should have coauthored at least one peer-reviewed research publication in a relevant area. If no coauthor meets this criterion, indicate this when submitting to request an exemption. Nominations are subject to organizer approval.
