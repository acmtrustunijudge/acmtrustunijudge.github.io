Universal Judges at ACM TRUST 2027

Reliable judging across languages and modalities

UniJudge is a full-day workshop at ACM TRUST 2027 asking whether AI judges stay reliable as the systems they evaluate move across languages, modalities and representations — spanning multilingual and low-resource judges, multimodal judges, their intersection, and cross-representation consistency.

Motivation

AI judges are becoming a general-purpose evaluation layer: they benchmark models, select checkpoints, gate safety evaluations and inform deployment decisions. Yet most judge methods and benchmarks remain concentrated in English and text-only settings.

As AI systems expand across languages and modalities, it is unclear whether judge reliability transfers with them. A judge that performs well on English text may behave differently in another language, fail to use the perceptual evidence it needs, or reward presentation rather than underlying quality.

These failure modes are studied separately across trustworthy AI, multilingual NLP, vision, speech and multimodal learning. UniJudge brings those communities together to treat the evaluator itself as an object of auditing and assurance — comparing failure modes, sharing evaluation protocols and building a common research agenda.

Topics

I

Multilingual and low-resource judges

  • Cross-lingual consistency and transfer
  • Low-resource and underrepresented languages
  • Code-switching and transliteration
  • Culturally dependent criteria
II

Multimodal judges

  • Image, document, audio, video and interactive outputs
  • Groundedness and hallucination
  • Reasoning and evidence use
III

Multilingual–multimodal evaluation

  • Multilingual vision–language and document systems
  • Speech and audio across languages
  • Transfer across both boundaries
IV

Cross-representation consistency

  • Translation and modality conversion
  • Formatting, verbosity and ordering
  • Counterfactual and perturbation tests

We also welcome work on judge auditing and meta-evaluation, reasoning validity, robustness, bias and calibration, causal faithfulness, multi-judge systems and disagreement, and benchmarks and evaluation protocols with clear implications for these settings.

Call for papers

We invite submissions on the reliability of AI judges in multilingual or multimodal settings, and on broader questions — reasoning validity, robustness, calibration, bias, causal faithfulness, judge disagreement, multi-judge systems — with clear implications for these settings. Negative results, replications, preliminary findings and works in progress are explicitly welcome.

Full papers

Completed research: new methods, analyses, benchmarks, datasets or evaluation protocols for auditing and meta-evaluating judges.

Short papers and extended abstracts

Position papers, negative results, replications, preliminary findings and works in progress.

Junior Spotlight Track

Either length

Dedicated oral slots for students and early-career researchers, with optional pre-workshop feedback from senior researchers. Indicate eligibility when you submit.

Format

PDF in the 2-column ACM sigconf template, following the TRUST 2027 author guidelines. References and the GenAI Usage Disclosure section do not count toward the page limit.

Review

Double-blind. Remove names, affiliations and identifying acknowledgments, and write self-references in the third person. Each submission receives at least two independent reviews from reviewers matched by expertise; organizers recuse themselves from conflicted submissions.

Presentation

At least one author of each accepted paper must register for ACM TRUST 2027 and present the work. The conference is hybrid, and remote presentation follows the conference’s virtual participation policies.

Open access

ACM publications are fully open access. Papers in the proceedings are covered by ACM Open if the corresponding author’s institution participates; otherwise an article processing charge applies. See the APC details.

Accepted workshop papers will be considered for publication in the TRUST 2027 conference proceedings. Papers selected for inclusion in the proceedings may undergo an additional light review to determine their suitability for publication.
